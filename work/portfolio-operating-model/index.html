The operating model

Building the operating model behind a growing portfolio

How a development function grew with its portfolio: ownership, a shared lifecycle and infrastructure that many products can lean on.

Status
Operating practices in use · Capability model evolving
My role
Development leadership, portfolio priorities and coordination across specialist teams, leadership and field operations.

PortfolioOrg designDevelopment lifecycle

The challenge

Expanding a technology education portfolio adds more than content. Each new offering brings an audience, instructors, support needs, systems and measurement requirements. When several products depend on the same people and expertise, growth also requires a change in how work is managed.

My contribution

I led the development function as its programs and products expanded. My work included team structure, priorities, work planning and delivery reviews. I connected leadership, domain leads, technology and content teams, and field operations.

The choices that shaped the work

We developed a product vocabulary for learning programs: audience, need, value, delivery model and improvement cycle. The development lifecycle connected definition and production with delivery, debriefing and adaptation.

We also established specialist and product responsibilities alongside shared infrastructure. As the portfolio evolved, we began shaping cross-program expertise so that knowledge developed in one track could support others. This capability model continued to develop through practice.

What changed

The portfolio expanded, the team structure evolved and dedicated leadership roles were added. Alongside those changes, we defined a shared development lifecycle and gave the relationship between content, systems and measurement an explicit place in the function’s work.

The result was a clearer portfolio structure: domain ownership, shared infrastructure and a path for delivery insights to return to planning. The cross-program expertise model continued to develop in response to emerging needs.

What I learned

Growth increases dependencies between teams. Local ownership and shared capabilities have to develop together. The leadership decision is where to give a team room to adapt, and where a common standard helps the whole organization work together.
