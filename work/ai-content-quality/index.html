Designing quality into AI-assisted content production

A production pilot for AI-assisted learning materials, built around one question: when is something that looks finished actually ready to use?

Status
Pilot and process design · Broader rollout not established
My role
Pilot initiation, output standards and review across content, pedagogy and design.

AI in productionContent QAProcess design

The challenge

A lesson needs to exist as a presentation, learner material, an instructor guide and practice. Producing each independently adds editing work and creates consistency risks. AI can accelerate generation, but a polished artifact does not establish technical accuracy or teaching usability.

My contribution

I initiated a pilot to rework existing learning materials with AI and defined the need for a repeatable production process. I called for comparative review: what was preserved, changed or added, and which issues travelled between outputs. The review used AI tools alongside documented technical checks.

The choices that shaped the work

We designed a shared content foundation from which different outputs could be produced while retaining their distinct purposes: live instruction, independent reading, practice and instructor decision support.

Review extended to the source material itself. Faithfully reproducing an error can spread it across several formats. Requirements covered concepts and examples, alignment between goals and tasks, rendering, and separation of questions from answers. These were process requirements, not evidence of an already complete automated system.

What the pilot produced

The work produced revised artifacts, production instructions and a detailed comparative review. The review identified improvements in organization and consistency alongside conceptual errors, assessment gaps and display problems. Findings became correction requirements and acceptance criteria for subsequent iterations.

The outcome at this stage is a more explicit quality process. Consistent time savings, improved learning outcomes and completion of all corrections have not yet been established.

What I learned

The easier it becomes to generate something that looks finished, the more clearly we need to define readiness for use. Working with AI sharpened the distinction between generation, review and release, with evidence required at each stage.
